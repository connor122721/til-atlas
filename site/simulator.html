Clone simulator

Take a real patient's tumor T cell repertoire — every clone's size and how many of its cells are stem-like, effector or exhausted, measured by single-cell RNA and TCR sequencing — infuse it into a virtual patient, and watch each clone over a year. The question is Krishna et al.'s: why do stem-like CD39⁻CD69⁻ cells predict durable response? The starting product is real data; what happens after infusion is a model.

What is measured and what is modeled

Measured (Wu et al. 2020, real data): for each of 14 patients, the 80 largest CD8⁺ T cell clones in the tumor, how many of each clone's cells look stem-like (S), effector (E) or terminally exhausted (X) from their RNA, whether the clone is also found in adjacent normal tissue or blood, and two independent flags for tumor reactivity: a TCR-only flag (expanded in the tumor and at least 4× more frequent there than in normal tissue) and an expression flag (the CXCL13⁺ CD39⁺ program). Clone sizes are scaled so the product holds the chosen number of cells.

Modeled: everything after infusion. Avidity is not measured, so flagged clones draw one at random around the slider value. A real TIL product is grown from the tumor for weeks, which reshapes it; using the tumor repertoire as the product is the simplest honest stand-in until a public cohort with sequenced infusion products is loaded.

Each clone has three compartments and every event (division, death, differentiation) is a Poisson draw each quarter-day, so small clones can disappear by chance.

antigen drive  A_i = r_i · a_i · Tu / (Tu + K_T)
               r_i ∈ {0,1} tumor-reactive, a_i functional avidity
homeostatic    H(t) = h0 · e^(−t/7)            (only after lymphodepletion)

S  self-renew  ρ_S (H + IL2 + 0.05 A) · crowd    S → S + S
   asymmetric  ρ_A · A                         S → S + E
   lose stem   δ · A        die  μ_S (slow)
E  divide      ρ_E (H + IL2 + A) · crowd
   exhaust     ε · A        die  μ_E (fast)    E → X
X  die         μ_X          kills at 30% of E strength

tumor   dTu/dt = g·Tu·(1 − Tu/Tmax) − k·Tu · K / (K + Kh·resistance)
        K = Σ_i a_i (E_i + 0.3 X_i)

The behavior that matters: effectors do the killing but die within weeks, so a product's initial effector mass buys a response, while its reactive stem-like pool decides whether new effectors keep coming once the infused ones are gone. Lower the stem-like share with everything else fixed and you'll usually see a partial response followed by relapse.

Rate parameters are set by hand to reproduce qualitative patterns from the TIL literature; they are not fitted to patient data. A good exercise: fit them to real post-infusion clonotype trajectories once a cohort such as Chiffelle et al. 2024 is loaded (see Learn).

Benchmarks: Rohaan et al., N Engl J Med 2022 (TIL vs ipilimumab, phase 3: objective response 49% vs 21%, complete response 20% vs 7%, 84 patients per arm); pooled C-144-01 analysis of lifileucel, J Immunother Cancer 2022 (153 patients: 8 complete and 40 partial responses).
