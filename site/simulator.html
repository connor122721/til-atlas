Does product stemness predict response?

Each patient's product varies around the sliders, like real manufacturing runs. This is the Krishna analysis, on virtual patients.

Expect more responses here than in real TIL trials, which report objective responses in roughly a third to a half of patients: the model has no HLA loss, no antigen-negative escape and no manufacturing failures. Adding one of those is a good first modification.

What the model is (and isn't)

Each of 80 infused clones has three compartments: S stem-like (TCF7⁺, the CD39⁻CD69⁻ “DN” cells), E effector, and X terminally exhausted (CD39⁺CD69⁺ “DP”). Cells are counted in units of 10⁶ and every event (division, death, differentiation) is a Poisson draw each quarter-day, so small clones can disappear by chance.

antigen drive  A_i = r_i · a_i · Tu / (Tu + K_T)
               r_i ∈ {0,1} tumor-reactive, a_i functional avidity
homeostatic    H(t) = h0 · e^(−t/7)            (only after lymphodepletion)

S  self-renew  ρ_S (H + IL2 + 0.05 A) · crowd    S → S + S
   asymmetric  ρ_A · A                         S → S + E
   lose stem   δ · A        die  μ_S (slow)
E  divide      ρ_E (H + IL2 + A) · crowd
   exhaust     ε · A        die  μ_E (fast)    E → X
X  die         μ_X          kills at 30% of E strength

tumor   dTu/dt = g·Tu·(1 − Tu/Tmax) − k·Tu · K / (K + Kh·resistance)
        K = Σ_i a_i (E_i + 0.3 X_i)

The behavior that matters: effectors do the killing but die within weeks, so a product's initial effector mass buys a response, while its reactive stem-like pool decides whether new effectors keep coming once the infused ones are gone. Lower the stem-like share with everything else fixed and you'll usually see a partial response followed by relapse.

Parameters are illustrative, set by hand to reproduce qualitative patterns reported in the TIL literature; they are not fitted to patient data. A good exercise: fit them to real clonotype trajectories (see the Learn page).
