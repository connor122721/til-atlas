Where are tumor clones expanded?

In many patients, clones expanded in the tumor were also expanded in the adjacent normal tissue — a sign that tumors are restocked with T cells from outside. Wu et al.'s headline result is that this kind of peripheral expansion associates with tumor infiltration and with response to immunotherapy. About the data

Wu et al., Nature 2020 (GEO GSE139555): 141,623 T cells from tumor, adjacent normal tissue and, for four patients, blood; 14 patients with lung, endometrial, colorectal or kidney cancer, untreated at surgery. Cell clusters and clonotypes are the authors'. Stem-like and terminal scores use the gene lists in analysis/signatures/; a clone is “predicted tumor-reactive” when its cells strongly express CXCL13, ENTPD1 (CD39), ITGAE (CD103), PDCD1, TNFRSF9 (4-1BB), HAVCR2, LAYN and TOX — a transcriptional prediction, not a functional assay.

Maynard et al., Cell 2020 (PRJNA591860): Smart-seq2 of lung cancer biopsies before targeted therapy, at residual disease and at progression, with B and T cell receptors reconstructed by BraCeR and TraCeR. Biopsies with at least 10 receptors are plotted.

Both arrive as processed files from the scirpy project's GitHub releases; analysis/datasets/wu2020/prepare.py and analysis/datasets/maynard2020/prepare.py rebuild everything here. Krishna et al. 2020 did not deposit their single-cell data, so the infusion-product comparison waits for a public cohort with products and outcomes (see Datasets).
